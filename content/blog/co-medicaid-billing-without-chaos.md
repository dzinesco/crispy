# What a Colorado Medicaid Billing Clearinghouse Actually Does

If you run a home care agency in Colorado, “billing” is not a form you fill out once a month. It is a pipeline: visits happen, EVV evidence has to line up, claims have to be shaped the way the payer expects, and money only shows up after the rejects stop.

A **billing clearinghouse** (or billing service that acts like one) sits in the middle of that pipeline. It is not your EHR. It is not your payroll. It is the layer that takes operational truth and turns it into payer-ready files — then chases the junk that comes back.

## The problem agencies actually feel

Most agencies do not lose money because caregivers did not work. They lose money because:

- visits do not match what the claim needs
- EVV and the claim tell different stories
- 837s bounce for fixable but invisible reasons
- someone is emailing spreadsheets and hoping

Hope is not a revenue cycle.

## What “clearinghouse” means here

In Colorado Medicaid context, a useful clearinghouse-style service usually does four jobs:

1. **Intake** — take clean operational exports (or structured claim files) from the agency
2. **Normalize** — map them into the shapes HCPF / trading partners expect
3. **Submit & track** — push claims and watch acknowledgements / rejects
4. **Close the loop** — tell the agency what failed, why, and what to fix next

That can be software. It can also be a **service** with a secure file drop — agencies post files, experts (and tools) process them, and status comes back without a 47-email thread.

## Why file intake beats email

Email is where PHI, versions, and accountability go to die. A productized intake (portal or secure drop) gives you:

- one place files land
- timestamps and versions
- less “which attachment was final?”
- a cleaner audit trail when something disputes later

If you already feel this pain, you are not alone. Colorado home care is operationally hard. The agencies that win treat billing like a system, not a personality.
