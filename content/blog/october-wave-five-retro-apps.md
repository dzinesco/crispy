While everyone else is shipping AI agents that book hotels and sort inbox, I spent the weekend putting **Windows 98**, a **BBS**, **Mavis Beacon**, **GeoCities**, and **DOOM** in your browser tab.

No install. No account. No "talk to sales." Just the old internet, working.

## What landed on October 12

Five playable apps, all plain JavaScript (or close enough), all already live on the [portfolio](https://crispygoat.com/portfolio):

1. **[CG-OS](https://crispygoat.com/demo/cg-os/)** — a full Windows 98 desktop. Boot it, drag windows, open Word, Excel, Paint, Minesweeper, Solitaire, Winamp from the Start menu.
2. **[DuskNet BBS](https://crispygoat.com/demo/dusknet/)** — late-90s bulletin board energy. Five boards, seeded threads, BBCode, quote-to-reply, sticky/locked, LED hit counter. Pick a handle and post.
3. **[Mavis Beacon Teaches Typing](https://crispygoat.com/demo/mavisbeacon/)** — twenty home-row lessons, timed tests, a falling-letter game, and that color-coded EGA keyboard.
4. **[GeoGoated](https://crispygoat.com/geogoated/)** — a working GeoCities clone. Claim a `~handle`, drag marquees and guestbooks around, leave the under-construction GIF up forever.
5. **[DOOM](https://crispygoat.com/demo/doom/)** — raycast E1M1 in the browser. WASD, mouse look, pistol, CRT glow.

Goatshop 5.0 is still the flagship. These five just filled the shelf.

## Why this, while the market is all "agents"

The feed this week is full of AI agents for small business: Muse, Zapier Agents, Copilot, Gemini, the usual "it takes a job off your plate" pitch. Fine. Useful, even.

But the web got dull when everything became a dashboard. Crispygoat is the opposite bet: a personal homepage that acts like 1998 on purpose. Western Slope nerd energy. Build things you can *play*, not pitch decks about automation.

If your business needs an agent, use the one that comes with the tools you already pay for. If you want to remember why the web was fun, open CG-OS and lose an hour to Minesweeper.
